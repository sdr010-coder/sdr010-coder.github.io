Two kinds of teams find me useful

I sit between an AI company and an enterprise. One side has capability and no process. The other has process and no capability. My job is the seam.

Track A — an AI company

Your model is ready. Your customers' processes are not.

You can ship capability faster than a large enterprise can absorb it. What blocks adoption is never the model — it is the customer's process, permissions, approvals and the fact that nobody can prove afterwards what the agent did.

I am the person who goes to that customer, models the process, puts the gates in the right places, and gets one value stream into production inside a quarter. I have done it from the enterprise side for 25 years, which means I know which questions to ask before the second meeting and which promises not to make.

forward-deployed engineer · enterprise AI solutions architect · AI delivery lead · technical program manager, agent platform · agent governance / product

Track B — an enterprise starting AI transformation

You do not need another pilot. You need one value stream that survives an audit.

Most programmes stall between "the demo worked" and "the business accepted the result". I design and run the layer that closes that gap: which agent may act, where a named person has to sign, what evidence is kept, and who owns the consequence.

Ninety days, one value stream, one gate a real person signs, one executive report generated from the record rather than typed by a person. A day-30 checkpoint decides whether we expand or stop — and I publish the stop rule before we start.

AI transformation PMO lead · head of AI adoption · digital transformation lead · program director, AI enablement · 企业 AI 落地负责人

Track C — if AI is not yet on the agenda

The same work, under its older name.

Many enterprises are not ready to hire for "AI transformation" and should not pretend to be. They are still doing ERP, shared services and process standardisation — and that is where the value is anyway, because an agent cannot improve a process nobody has defined.

Plenty of my twenty-five years sits here, and I am comfortable being hired for it: global SAP programme delivery, PMO build-out, GBS transition, operating-model change. AI enters through the door those programmes open, not through a separate initiative.

digital transformation director · global process transformation lead · global IT / ERP transformation director · program director

Why the combination is rare

AI companies hire people who understand models. Enterprises hire people who understand process. Very few people have spent a career in the second and then built real things in the first.

  • Twenty-five years inside regulated operations — food, medical imaging, medical devices, industrial, lighting, robotics — running SAP programmes and PMOs, not advising them.
  • A PMO platform designed and built from zero to live in 4.5 months as PMO: 56 pages, 51 tables, 119,000 lines, 1,485 processes, a 455-item category dictionary underneath. Built with AI coding; I owned the model, the definitions and the acceptance.
  • A two-week agent-governance proof of concept: 475 processes, an 8-step journey, 6 fail-closed gates, a dual ledger with a hash chain. Approve, stop, audit — not slideware.
  • The published position behind it: Enterprise Harness, with eleven documents and a runnable reference implementation of 83 tests.
